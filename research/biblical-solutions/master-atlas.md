# Master Atlas: Biblical Problems → Modern Solutions

## 1. Famine and food insecurity

**Biblical references:** Genesis 41–47; Ruth 1; 1 Kings 17; Joel 1.

**Biblical intervention:** Joseph identifies a future famine, stores surplus during abundant years, and distributes food during scarcity (Genesis 41).

**Underlying principle:** Forecast scarcity and convert abundance into resilience before the crisis.

**Modern problem:** Food insecurity, price shocks, conflict disruption, climate shocks, storage and distribution failures.

**Possible modern solution:** Predictive food-resilience infrastructure that combines weather, crop, inventory, market, logistics and conflict signals to forecast shortages and connect surplus to deficit regions.

**Product hypothesis:** FoodResilience.

---

## 2. Personal debt

**Biblical reference:** 2 Kings 4:1–7.

**Biblical intervention:** A widow facing creditor action is directed to use an existing productive asset (oil), expand production, sell it, repay the debt, and live from the remainder.

**Underlying principle:** Rescue from debt can involve activating productive capacity rather than simply issuing more debt.

**Modern problem:** Predatory lending, debt cycles, emergency borrowing, high-interest digital loans.

**Possible solution:** Asset- and skill-based debt recovery that maps a person's productive resources to realistic income opportunities and repayment plans.

**Product hypothesis:** DebtBridge.

---

## 3. Poverty and food access

**Biblical references:** Leviticus 19:9–10; Deuteronomy 24:19–22; Ruth 2.

**Biblical intervention:** Gleaning rules leave part of agricultural output accessible to vulnerable people.

**Underlying principle:** Economic systems can deliberately preserve access to unused resources while allowing people to participate with dignity.

**Modern problem:** Poverty, food waste, unused inventory, unemployment.

**Possible solution:** A surplus-to-opportunity network connecting unused food, materials, equipment, space and temporary work with verified demand.

**Product hypothesis:** Surplus/OpenHarvest.

---

## 4. Debt traps

**Biblical reference:** Deuteronomy 15; Leviticus 25.

**Biblical intervention:** Periodic debt-release and economic reset mechanisms are embedded in the covenant economic framework.

**Underlying principle:** Debt should not automatically become a permanent, self-reinforcing condition.

**Modern problem:** Compounding debt, penalty cycles, refinancing traps.

**Possible solution:** Debt-risk monitoring that detects unsustainable trajectories and triggers restructuring, creditor negotiation, income intervention and financial coaching before default.

**Product hypothesis:** Debt Reset Engine.

---

## 5. Dishonest commerce and measurement

**Biblical references:** Leviticus 19:35–36; Proverbs 11:1; Amos 8:5.

**Biblical intervention:** Accurate weights and measures are required; dishonest scales are condemned.

**Underlying principle:** Transactions require trustworthy measurement.

**Modern problem:** Fake reviews, counterfeit goods, fabricated metrics, deceptive pricing, false business claims, AI-generated evidence.

**Possible solution:** Digital verification/provenance infrastructure for claims, products, credentials, metrics and transactions.

**Product hypothesis:** TruthMark.

---

## 6. False testimony and unreliable claims

**Biblical references:** Exodus 20:16; Deuteronomy 19:15; Deuteronomy 13; 1 John 4:1.

**Biblical intervention:** False witness is prohibited and important claims are to be tested rather than blindly accepted; multiple witnesses can establish a matter.

**Underlying principle:** Important decisions should not depend on an unverified claim or single source.

**Modern problem:** Misinformation, deepfakes, fabricated evidence, fake reviews, synthetic media.

**Possible solution:** Evidence graphs that connect a claim to primary documents, independent sources, timestamps and corroboration.

**Product hypothesis:** ClaimCheck / Evidence Graph.

---

## 7. Worker exploitation and withheld wages

**Biblical references:** Deuteronomy 24:14–15; Jeremiah 22:13; James 5:4.

**Biblical intervention:** Workers are entitled to fair and timely compensation.

**Underlying principle:** Labor creates an obligation that should be visible, measurable and enforceable.

**Modern problem:** Wage theft, delayed salary, hidden deductions, gig-worker disputes, contract abuse.

**Possible solution:** Worker contract/payment protection with clear obligations, proof of work, payment tracking and dispute evidence.

**Product hypothesis:** FairPay.

---

## 8. Corrupt or overloaded governance

**Biblical reference:** Exodus 18:13–26.

**Biblical intervention:** Jethro tells Moses that centralized handling of every dispute is unsustainable. Capable leaders are appointed at different levels, with difficult cases escalated.

**Underlying principle:** Complex systems need distributed authority, clear responsibility and escalation.

**Modern problem:** Bureaucratic bottlenecks, institutional capture, overloaded leaders, weak accountability.

**Possible solution:** Distributed governance and approval infrastructure for governments, companies and autonomous systems.

**Product hypothesis:** Delegated Governance Layer.

---

## 9. Property and land abuse

**Biblical references:** Isaiah 5:8; 1 Kings 21; Leviticus 25.

**Biblical intervention/principle:** Land and inheritance receive protection; Naboth's property is a central example of abuse of political power to acquire private land.

**Modern problem:** Land fraud, disputed ownership, land grabbing, property concentration.

**Possible solution:** Property provenance and ownership-risk verification before transactions.

**Product hypothesis:** LandProof.

---

## 10. Dispute escalation

**Biblical reference:** Matthew 18:15–17.

**Biblical intervention:** Address a conflict privately first, then involve additional witnesses/community, with escalation when reconciliation fails.

**Underlying principle:** Resolve problems at the lowest appropriate level before escalating.

**Modern problem:** Expensive and slow dispute resolution.

**Possible solution:** Structured AI-assisted negotiation, mediation and evidence preparation before formal arbitration or court.

**Product hypothesis:** Resolve.

---

## 11. Community support

**Biblical references:** Acts 2:44–45; Acts 4:32–35.

**Biblical intervention:** Early Christian communities voluntarily shared resources so people in need were supported.

**Underlying principle:** Community can pool resources and respond to verified need.

**Modern problem:** Emergency expenses, medical bills, education costs, food insecurity.

**Possible solution:** Transparent mutual-aid infrastructure with verified need, contribution tracking and distribution records.

**Product hypothesis:** Community Pool.

---

## 12. Stewardship and ecological restoration

**Biblical references:** Genesis 2:15; Leviticus 25.

**Biblical intervention/principle:** Humans are given responsibility to cultivate and keep the land; agricultural land receives periods of rest.

**Modern problem:** Resource depletion, soil degradation, deforestation and ecological loss.

**Possible solution:** Regeneration accounting that measures extraction, ecological impact and verified restoration.

**Product hypothesis:** Regeneration Ledger.

---

## 13. Vulnerable outsiders and displaced people

**Biblical references:** Leviticus 19:33–34; Deuteronomy 10:18–19; Ruth.

**Biblical intervention:** Foreigners are explicitly included in protections and social obligations.

**Underlying principle:** Displacement should not erase a person's ability to participate in society.

**Modern problem:** Refugees and migrants struggle with identity, employment, housing, education and financial access.

**Possible solution:** Portable, consent-based credentials for verified identity, skills, qualifications and work history.

**Product hypothesis:** NewStart.

---

## 14. Health and disease prevention

**Biblical references:** Leviticus 13–15; Numbers 5.

**Biblical intervention:** Ancient Israel used separation and cleanliness practices around certain diseases and bodily conditions.

**Underlying principle:** Prevention and containment can reduce harm before treatment.

**Modern problem:** Infectious disease outbreaks, food contamination, antimicrobial resistance.

**Possible solution:** Early-warning systems combining health, environmental, laboratory and mobility signals.

**Product hypothesis:** Prevention Intelligence.

---

## 15. Crisis preparation

**Biblical references:** Genesis 41; Proverbs 6:6–8.

**Biblical intervention/principle:** Prepare during favorable conditions rather than waiting for scarcity.

**Modern problem:** Organizations and households often respond after a crisis begins.

**Possible solution:** AI systems that identify leading indicators and recommend preparation actions.

**Product hypothesis:** Crisis Readiness Engine.

---

## 16. Economic inequality

**Biblical references:** Isaiah 5:8; Deuteronomy 15; Leviticus 25; James 5.

**Biblical mechanisms:** Gleaning, debt release, property/inheritance rules, generosity and warnings against extreme accumulation.

**Underlying principle:** Economic systems should prevent vulnerability from becoming permanently self-reinforcing.

**Modern problem:** Asset concentration, unequal access to capital, persistent poverty.

**Possible solution:** Economic mobility infrastructure combining debt restructuring, savings, skills, productive assets and market access.

**Product hypothesis:** Economic Reset / Escape.

---

## 17. Authority boundaries and autonomous action

**Biblical principles:** Exodus 18; Deuteronomy 19; Proverbs on accountability; New Testament governance/community practices.

**Underlying principle:** Authority should be bounded, reviewable and escalated when stakes are high.

**Modern problem:** Autonomous AI agents can potentially spend money, modify systems, send communications or execute transactions.

**Possible solution:** Permissioning, risk classification, approval thresholds, audit trails, quarantine and emergency controls.

**Product hypothesis:** Agent Governance Layer.

**Existing Problem Radar adjacency:** IntentGuard already explores fail-closed pre-execution evaluation for autonomous digital assets and agent workflows.

# 21. Biblical solution philosophy

Across the atlas, recurring intervention mechanisms include:

1. **Forecast** — identify future risk.
2. **Prepare** — build resilience before crisis.
3. **Reserve** — preserve surplus for scarcity.
4. **Redistribute** — move unused resources toward need.
5. **Reset** — prevent debt or disadvantage from becoming permanent.
6. **Verify** — establish trustworthy measurements and claims.
7. **Corroborate** — avoid relying on one unverified witness.
8. **Delegate** — distribute authority according to capability.
9. **Escalate** — send high-risk or unresolved matters to higher authority.
10. **Protect** — safeguard vulnerable people and property.
11. **Reconcile** — resolve disputes before they become larger conflicts.
12. **Restore** — regenerate resources rather than endlessly extracting them.
13. **Include** — integrate outsiders and vulnerable people into society.
14. **Account** — make obligations and decisions visible.
15. **Limit authority** — prevent unchecked power.

These mechanisms are the core intellectual property of the research layer—not as proprietary biblical ideas, but as the organizing framework Problem Radar can use to generate and compare modern solution hypotheses.
