# Research Methodology

## Objective

Turn biblical problem-solving material into testable modern solution hypotheses without making unsupported prophetic claims.

## Step 1 — Extract the biblical problem

Record:

- passage
- people affected
- trigger
- consequences
- relevant social/economic structure.

## Step 2 — Identify the intervention

Ask:

- What action was taken?
- Who took it?
- Was it a command, law, narrative response, warning or consequence?
- Was the intervention actually presented as desirable?

Do not treat every biblical event as a recommended solution.

## Step 3 — Identify the mechanism

Strip away ancient context.

Example:

**Joseph's grain storage**

becomes:

> forecast → reserve → distribute.

## Step 4 — Identify the modern analogue

Do not force a match.

The modern problem must be independently documented.

## Step 5 — Identify what has changed

Compare:

- technology
- scale
- institutions
- law
- incentives
- culture
- information flow.

## Step 6 — Design the modern intervention

Ask:

> If the underlying principle still makes sense, what would implementing it with modern technology look like?

## Step 7 — Validate

A solution is not validated because it has a biblical precedent.

Test:

- customer pain
- frequency
- urgency
- existing alternatives
- willingness to pay
- operational feasibility
- legal/regulatory constraints
- measurable outcomes.

## Step 8 — Score

Suggested Problem Radar score:

`Opportunity = Severity × Frequency × SolutionGap × WillingnessToPay × Feasibility`

Normalize each factor to 1–10.

Add separate evidence confidence rather than hiding uncertainty.

## Evidence levels

### A — Explicit
The biblical text explicitly establishes the intervention.

### B — Strong inference
The narrative clearly demonstrates a mechanism, but does not prescribe it universally.

### C — Principle
A broader ethical/institutional principle is inferred.

### D — Speculative
The modern connection is exploratory and requires validation.

Only A–C should normally influence the core atlas. D belongs in an experimental section.

## Modern evidence

Modern claims should be sourced independently using high-quality evidence such as:

- UN
- World Bank
- WHO
- FAO
- UNHCR
- UNCTAD
- ILO
- national statistics
- peer-reviewed research
- reputable market research.

## Important limitation

This project is not designed to prove:

- that every modern event was predicted by Scripture;
- that a modern technology is a biblical prophecy;
- that every biblical institution should be copied today;
- that a biblical intervention is automatically economically effective today.

It is a **solution-generation and pattern-research framework**.
