# Biblical Solution Mechanisms

This file extracts reusable mechanisms rather than individual stories.

## 1. Forecast

**Canonical example:** Joseph — Genesis 41.

**Pattern:** Detect future risk → quantify it → act during the favorable period.

**Modern applications:** food, finance, climate, cybersecurity, healthcare, supply chains.

---

## 2. Reserve

**Canonical example:** Joseph's grain storage.

**Pattern:** Convert temporary abundance into a buffer.

**Modern applications:** food reserves, emergency funds, compute capacity, medicine stockpiles, critical materials.

---

## 3. Productive rescue

**Canonical example:** Widow's oil — 2 Kings 4.

**Pattern:** Identify existing productive assets → activate them → generate cash flow → resolve immediate liability.

**Modern applications:** debt recovery, unemployment, microenterprise.

---

## 4. Gleaning / controlled access to surplus

**Canonical examples:** Leviticus 19:9–10; Ruth 2.

**Pattern:** Deliberately leave part of productive output accessible to vulnerable people.

**Modern applications:** food waste, surplus inventory, unused capacity, community employment.

---

## 5. Reset

**Canonical examples:** Deuteronomy 15; Leviticus 25.

**Pattern:** Prevent an economic liability from becoming permanently self-reinforcing.

**Modern applications:** debt restructuring, bankruptcy design, economic mobility.

---

## 6. Honest measurement

**Canonical examples:** Leviticus 19:35–36; Proverbs 11:1.

**Pattern:** Make measurement accurate and auditable.

**Modern applications:** digital claims, product authenticity, financial metrics, AI outputs, marketplace trust.

---

## 7. Multiple witnesses

**Canonical example:** Deuteronomy 19:15.

**Pattern:** High-consequence claims should be corroborated.

**Modern applications:** journalism, insurance, AI fact checking, legal evidence, scientific claims.

---

## 8. Distributed authority

**Canonical example:** Exodus 18.

**Pattern:** Delegate routine decisions; escalate exceptional decisions.

**Modern applications:** enterprise governance, government, AI agents, security operations.

---

## 9. Property protection

**Canonical examples:** 1 Kings 21; Leviticus 25.

**Pattern:** Protect legitimate ownership against stronger actors.

**Modern applications:** land registries, digital property, intellectual property, asset provenance.

---

## 10. Graduated dispute resolution

**Canonical example:** Matthew 18:15–17.

**Pattern:** Private resolution → witnesses → community/authority → final escalation.

**Modern applications:** workplace disputes, marketplace disputes, landlord/tenant issues, online communities.

---

## 11. Mutual aid

**Canonical examples:** Acts 2:44–45; Acts 4:32–35.

**Pattern:** Community members pool resources and respond to need.

**Modern applications:** emergency funds, healthcare, education, housing, local resilience.

---

## 12. Stewardship and rest

**Canonical examples:** Genesis 2:15; Leviticus 25.

**Pattern:** Resource use must account for regeneration.

**Modern applications:** regenerative agriculture, forestry, water management, corporate environmental accounting.

---

## 13. Inclusion of outsiders

**Canonical examples:** Leviticus 19:33–34; Deuteronomy 10:18–19.

**Pattern:** Vulnerable outsiders receive explicit protection.

**Modern applications:** refugee integration, migrant credentials, access to jobs and services.

---

## 14. Prevention and containment

**Canonical examples:** Leviticus 13–15; Numbers 5.

**Pattern:** Identify conditions that can spread harm and contain them early.

**Modern applications:** infectious disease, food safety, cyber threats, financial contagion.

---

## 15. Accountability

**Pattern across biblical law and narrative:** authority is repeatedly subjected to standards, witnesses, prophets, judges, communal rules and consequences.

**Modern applications:** corporate governance, government procurement, AI governance, public accountability.

---

# Design rule

Do not copy ancient institutions literally.

Translate the **mechanism**, then test whether it works under modern:

- technology
- law
- economics
- culture
- human behavior
- privacy
- scalability.
