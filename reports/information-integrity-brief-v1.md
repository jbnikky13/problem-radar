## Executive signal

Multiple Nigerian public institutions have issued recent warnings about false recruitment information using their names or purported recruitment processes. In August 2026, FCSC warned about unauthorised recruitment links and instructed applicants to verify announcements through its official channels. NUPRC separately warned about fake and AI-generated employment letters and reported alleged extortion connected to purported recruitment assistance. ## Why this matters

The recurring operational problem is not simply "scams exist." It is an **information-integrity gap**: legitimate institutions must repeatedly establish which information is authentic, while members of the public may encounter unofficial links, websites, messages or documents that imitate institutional communications.

## Information gap hypothesis

A public institution may know about a false notice only after it begins circulating or after complaints arrive. A cross-source monitoring layer could potentially identify repeated impersonation patterns, compare them with authoritative announcements, and measure the time between first detectable signal and official correction.

This is a hypothesis to validate with organizations; the public evidence does not establish that such a monitoring gap exists for every institution.

## Proposed intelligence product

**Information Integrity Monitor**

A recurring intelligence brief could contain:

- detected official-name/brand impersonation signals
- affected service or process
- source/channel type
- first-observed date
- recurrence and source diversity
- relationship to the institution's authoritative communications
- correction/response status
- evidence links
- confidence level
- unresolved questions

The commercial output would be aggregated intelligence. It would not include personal contact lists, identifiable complainants, private messages or individual behavioural profiles.

## Pilot design

**30-day pilot:** select one public-service information domain and produce weekly briefs using public and otherwise lawfully permitted sources.

Success should be measured by whether the buyer identifies the intelligence as:

1. new,
2. timely,
3. actionable, and
4. materially useful beyond existing monitoring.

## Buyer questions

Before selling a recurring service, ask:

- How does the organization currently discover false or impersonated information?
- Who owns the response?
- How long does verification normally take?
- Which channels are hardest to monitor?
- What evidence is required before issuing a public correction?
- What would an earlier signal change operationally?
- Would the organization pay for a weekly brief, dashboard, alert feed or API?

## Confidence

**Pattern confidence: medium-high.** Multiple current official sources independently document the phenomenon.

**Market-size confidence: low.** We do not yet have a defensible estimate of total incidents, financial loss or buyer willingness to pay.

**Product-market-fit confidence: untested.** No customer interview or paid pilot has yet validated the product hypothesis.

## Decision

The next step is **customer validation, not more feature development**. The evidence is sufficient to justify a focused pilot conversation, but not sufficient to claim a validated commercial market.
